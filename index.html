Mastering High-Mark Sentence Variety & Structures

To score in the highest mark band (Band 5: 8-9 Marks for Language), students must demonstrate a wide range of complex structures, correct punctuation, and varied sentence beginnings. Below is your complete color-coded toolkit.

The 4 Fundamental Sentence Structures

Language Variety Toolkit

1 Simple Sentence

Core

Contains one independent clause with a subject and verb. Use for strong dramatic impact or concise statements.

Formula & Example:

[The school council] [approved] [the new recycling initiative].

Subject + Verb + Object

2 Compound Sentence

FANBOYS

Connects two equal independent clauses using a coordinating conjunction (FANBOYS).

Formula & Example:

[The event was meticulously planned], yet [many students arrived late].

Main Clause 1 + FANBOYS + Main Clause 2

3 Complex Sentence

Subordinate

Joins an independent main clause with a dependent clause using subordinating conjunctions (Although, Because, Since, While).

Formula & Example:

Although [the weather was severe], [the team completed the challenge].

Subordinator + Dependent + Main Clause

4 Compound-Complex

Top Band Target

Combines at least two main independent clauses and at least one dependent clause.

Formula & Example:

Because [the hall was crowded], [many left early], and [the principal cut the speech].

Dependent + Main 1 + FANBOYS + Main 2

5 Participle Clause Openings

Vivid Variety

Start sentences with an -ing (Present Participle) or Having + V3 (Perfect Participle) to condense action and improve sophisticated flow.

Participle Example:

Facing continuous digital distractions, [students struggled to maintain deep study focus].

-ing Participle Action + Main Subject & Action

6 Formal Passive Voice

Report & Article Register

Shift focus from the action-doer to the receiver/object. Crucial for objective tone in Formal Reports & Formal Articles.

Passive Voice Example:

It was observed that several computers were damaged during the storm.

Impersonal Lead + Object + Was/Were + Past Participle (V3)

The Conditionals Matrix for Writing Tasks

Top Examiner Requirement
Zero Conditional Facts

If + Present, Present

"If schools promote recycling, waste decreases."

Use in: Formal Reports / Articles
1st Conditional Real Future

If + Present, Will + V1

"If you visit me next week, we will explore the coast."

Use in: Informal Emails / Advice
2nd Conditional Hypothetical

If + Past, Would + V1

"If we had more computers, students would study more."

Use in: Suggestions / Recommendations
3rd Conditional Past Regret

If + Past Perf, Would Have + V3

"If they had brought maps, they would not have got lost."

Use in: Reviews / Past Critique
